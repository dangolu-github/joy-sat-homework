Lesson 1 supplement: long sentence structure and boundary patterns

1 | Dependent Clause + Main Clause | Astronomy

Although the Hubble Space Telescope's primary mirror initially produced blurred images, astronauts installed corrective optics during a 1993 servicing mission, allowing the telescope to return sharper observations of distant galaxies, nebulae, and star-forming regions.

  • Structure: dependent clause + comma + independent clause.
  • SAT point: A clause beginning with although cannot stand alone, so a period or semicolon after it would be wrong.
2 | Independent Clause + Independent Clause: Semicolon | Science History

Marie Curie shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics for research on radiation; in 1911, she received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for her work on radium and polonium.

  • Structure: independent clause + semicolon + independent clause.
  • SAT point: A semicolon works only because both sides are complete sentences.
3 | Independent Clause + Independent Clause: Period | Archaeology

The Rosetta Stone was discovered in Egypt in 1799. Because the stone includes versions of a decree in multiple scripts, it helped scholars make progress in deciphering ancient Egyptian hieroglyphs.

  • Structure: complete sentence + period + complete sentence.
  • SAT point: If both sides are independent clauses, a period is one clean way to avoid a run-on or comma splice.
4 | Comma + FANBOYS | Marine Science

The Great Barrier Reef lies off the northeastern coast of Australia, and it is widely recognized as the world's largest coral reef system.

  • Structure: independent clause + comma + FANBOYS + independent clause.
  • SAT point: And alone is not enough when it joins two full sentences; the comma matters.
5 | No Punctuation: Preposition + Object | Geology

Basalt from volcanic eruptions is common in Iceland, where volcanic activity along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge has helped shape much of the island's landscape.

  • Structure: the preposition from stays attached to its object, volcanic eruptions.
  • SAT point: Do not insert a comma, colon, dash, or semicolon between a preposition and its object.
6 | No Punctuation: Verb + Object | Archaeology

At Pompeii, a Roman city buried by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 CE, archaeologists have uncovered streets, houses, frescoes, and everyday objects that preserve details of ancient urban life.

  • Structure: verb phrase + direct objects.
  • SAT point: A list after a verb does not automatically need a colon; punctuation cannot split uncovered from its objects.
7 | Nonessential Which-Clause | Agriculture

The Svalbard Global Seed Vault, which opened in Norway in 2008, stores backup copies of crop seeds so that plant varieties can be preserved for future research and recovery efforts.

  • Structure: main sentence + comma + nonessential which-clause + comma + main sentence continues.
  • SAT point: If a which clause gives extra information, it usually needs paired commas.
8 | Colon After a Complete Sentence | Space Exploration

The Apollo 11 mission achieved a milestone that had never before occurred in human history: astronauts walked on the Moon.

  • Structure: complete sentence + colon + explanation.
  • SAT point: The colon is allowed because the words before it form a complete sentence.
9 | Appositive / Nonessential Noun Phrase | Mathematics

Katherine Johnson, a mathematician whose calculations supported early U.S. crewed spaceflights, worked at NASA and its predecessor agency for more than three decades.

  • Structure: subject + comma + nonessential noun phrase + comma + predicate.
  • SAT point: A descriptive appositive needs matching punctuation on both sides.
10 | Complex List With Semicolons | Chemistry

The 2022 Nobel Prize in Chemistry recognized three researchers associated with click chemistry and bioorthogonal chemistry: Carolyn R. Bertozzi, a Stanford chemist; Morten Meldal, a Danish chemist; and K. Barry Sharpless, a two-time Nobel laureate.

  • Structure: complete sentence + colon + complex list separated by semicolons.
  • SAT point: Semicolons can separate list items when the items already contain commas.
11 | Direct Question After a Colon | Literature

In Things Fall Apart, Chinua Achebe's depiction of Umuofia raises a central question: How will a community's traditions be affected when outside religious and political forces arrive?

  • Structure: complete sentence + colon + direct question.
  • SAT point: A direct question after a colon should use question word order and end with a question mark.
12 | No Punctuation Between Subject and Verb | Literary Archives

The Octavia E. Butler papers at the Huntington Library contain manuscripts, photographs, notebooks, and research materials related to the science fiction writer's career.

  • Structure: long subject + verb + object.
  • SAT point: Do not place a comma, colon, dash, or semicolon between the full subject and its verb.
13 | Essential That-Clause | Social History

The oral histories that researchers collect from community members can preserve local experiences that may not appear in official records.

  • Structure: essential that clause inside the subject.
  • SAT point: Do not add commas around information that is necessary to identify the noun.
14 | Introductory Time Clause | Physics

When particles are suspended in a liquid, their random motion can be observed under a microscope, a phenomenon known as Brownian motion.

  • Structure: introductory dependent time clause + comma + independent clause.
  • SAT point: A when clause at the beginning usually takes a comma before the main sentence begins.
15 | Two Coordinated Noun Clauses: No Internal Punctuation | Oceanography

Using measurements collected over many years, oceanographers can study how currents move heat through the ocean and what effects that movement has on marine ecosystems.

  • Structure: verb + two coordinated noun clauses joined by and.
  • SAT point: When two parallel elements complete the same verb, no comma, colon, or semicolon is needed between them.
