SAT Reading and Writing - Lesson 1

Boundaries: how to decide punctuation from sentence structure

Big Idea

Boundary questions are structure questions. Do not choose punctuation by sound. First decide what kind of words are on each side of the blank.

Independent clause
A complete sentence idea: subject + conjugated verb + complete thought.
Dependent clause
Has a subject and verb, but begins with a dependent word like although, because, or when.
Phrase
A group of words without a full subject + conjugated verb structure.
Supplement
Extra information that can be removed without breaking the sentence.

1. Dependent Clause + Main Clause

A dependent clause cannot stand alone as a sentence. Common dependent words include although, because, when, while, if, since, unless, before, and after.

Dependent clause, independent clause.

Example: Although the experiment was small, the results were useful.
  • If the first part starts with a dependent word, it is not a complete sentence by itself.
  • Do not use a period, semicolon, or colon right after a dependent clause.
  • A comma is usually the correct boundary before the main clause.
Practice 1
Dependent Clause

Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?

B. asteroids, several
C. asteroids; several
D. asteroids: several

2. Independent Clause + Independent Clause

When both sides of the blank are complete sentences, they must be separated correctly.

Independent clause. Independent clause.

Independent clause; independent clause.

Independent clause, FANBOYS independent clause.

Example: The forecast changed overnight; the team updated its plan.
  • A comma alone cannot connect two complete sentences.
  • A coordinating conjunction alone is also not enough.
  • Use a period, a semicolon, or comma + FANBOYS.

3. Comma + FANBOYS

FANBOYS are coordinating conjunctions: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.

Independent clause, but independent clause.

Example: The evidence seemed weak, but later research supported the claim.
  • FANBOYS can connect two independent clauses.
  • When both sides are complete sentences, place a comma before the FANBOYS word.
  • The FANBOYS word also shows the logical relationship.

4. No Punctuation Inside One Grammar Unit

Sometimes the correct answer is no punctuation. Do not separate words that belong together.

Example: The researchers discovered tools, pottery, and coins.
  • Do not separate a preposition from its object.
  • Do not separate a verb from its object.
  • Long sentences do not automatically need extra punctuation.

5. Nonessential Information

Nonessential information adds detail, but the sentence still works if that information is removed.

Main sentence, extra information, main sentence continues.

Example: Brownian motion, which is random particle movement, can be observed in liquid.
  • Nonessential information can be removed without breaking the sentence.
  • It usually needs matching punctuation on both sides.
  • Use two commas, two dashes, or parentheses; do not mix punctuation styles.

A. movement: which
B. movement, which
C. movement which
D. movement. Which

6. Colon Preview

A colon can introduce an explanation, example, list, or identification.

Complete sentence: explanation.

Example: The team tried a new method: sequencing ancient DNA.
  • A colon must come after a complete sentence.
  • The information after the colon explains, identifies, or gives an example.
  • A colon is different from a semicolon: the second part does not have to be a complete sentence.
