Preview routine

  • Choose the sentence that fulfills the complete goal accurately, relevantly, and economically.

Question 1

Difficult

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • A coastal restoration team tested two approaches in neighboring damaged mangrove areas.
  • In one area, workers planted mangrove seedlings in evenly spaced rows.
  • Sixty-eight percent of the planted seedlings survived the first year, but only 21 percent remained after two severe storms.
  • In the other area, workers restored blocked tidal channels and allowed mangroves to recolonize naturally.
  • After three years, naturally recolonized mangroves covered 54 percent of that area and included four native species.
  • The planted area contained one mangrove species.
Skeleton: two approaches → early planted survival → later cover and diversityAnchor: emphasize why + long-term advantageScope lock: compare later outcomes, not merely describe the two methodsTrick watch: an accurate first-year statistic can still miss the requested time frame and comparison

The student wants to emphasize why restoring tidal flow and allowing natural recolonization may offer a long-term advantage over planting rows of seedlings. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. The team tested seedling planting in one damaged mangrove area and tidal-channel restoration in a neighboring area.
  2. B. Although 68 percent of the planted seedlings survived their first year, severe storms later occurred in the area.
  3. C. Restoring tidal channels allowed mangroves to recolonize naturally, whereas workers planted one species in evenly spaced rows elsewhere.
  4. D. After three years, the naturally recolonized area had greater mangrove cover and species diversity than the planted area, whose early survival gains largely disappeared after storms.

Question 2

Moderate

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Dr. Leila Hassan is a dendrochronologist.
  • Dendrochronology uses the annual growth rings in wood to study past environmental conditions and date wooden objects.
  • Hassan examines cedar beams preserved in centuries-old buildings.
  • Wide rings generally formed in years with more favorable growing conditions.
  • Hassan has used ring patterns in the beams to reconstruct changes in rainfall across central Anatolia.
Skeleton: scientist → field definition → one applicationAnchor: introduce Hassan and her field + unfamiliar audienceScope lock: include both the person and a clear explanation of the fieldTrick watch: a strong study detail may omit either the scientist or the definition the audience needs

The student wants to introduce Hassan and her field of study to an audience unfamiliar with dendrochronology. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. Cedar beams preserved in centuries-old buildings contain annual growth rings that vary in width.
  2. B. Dendrochronologist Dr. Leila Hassan studies annual growth rings in wood to date objects and reconstruct past environmental conditions, including rainfall in central Anatolia.
  3. C. Dr. Leila Hassan has reconstructed rainfall changes in central Anatolia by examining cedar beams from old buildings.
  4. D. Dendrochronology can reveal past environmental conditions because wider tree rings generally indicate more favorable growing conditions.

Question 3

Difficult

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • One study examined city sparrows living near roads with different traffic-noise levels.
  • Sparrows at noisier sites sang at lower pitches than sparrows at quieter sites.
  • The researchers proposed that lower-pitched songs traveled more reliably through the local sound environment.
  • A second study examined city sparrows exposed to different levels of artificial light at night.
  • Sparrows at brighter sites began their dawn songs earlier.
  • The second study found no consistent relationship between artificial light and song pitch.
Skeleton: study 1: noise → pitch; study 2: light → timing, not pitchAnchor: compare the effectsScope lock: keep the variable and outcome paired correctly in each studyTrick watch: two true details can still be joined with a relationship the notes never establish

The student wants to compare the effects that traffic noise and artificial light had on sparrow song in the two studies. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. Traffic noise was associated with lower-pitched sparrow songs, whereas artificial light was associated with earlier singing but not with a consistent change in pitch.
  2. B. Both traffic noise and artificial light caused sparrows to sing at lower pitches so that their songs would travel more reliably.
  3. C. Sparrows at brighter sites began singing earlier, while sparrows at noisier sites did not change when they began singing.
  4. D. The two studies found that urban conditions altered sparrow songs, although neither study identified which feature of song had changed.

Question 4

Moderate

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Deep-sea corals are difficult to survey directly over large areas.
  • A research team equipped an autonomous ocean glider with a system for collecting seawater samples.
  • The team analyzed environmental DNA, or eDNA, in the samples.
  • The glider traveled 420 kilometers along the continental slope.
  • The samples revealed traces of six deep-sea coral species.
  • Two of those species had not previously been recorded in that section of the slope.
Skeleton: glider collects water → eDNA analysis → six species, two new to areaAnchor: method and main findingScope lock: the answer must contain both halves of the goalTrick watch: a sentence can describe the method accurately and still omit the result

The student wants to present the study's method and its main finding. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. Because deep-sea corals are difficult to survey directly, researchers have begun testing autonomous ocean gliders.
  2. B. An autonomous glider traveled 420 kilometers along the continental slope while collecting seawater samples.
  3. C. By analyzing eDNA in seawater collected by an autonomous glider, researchers detected six deep-sea coral species, including two not previously recorded in the surveyed area.
  4. D. Environmental DNA in seawater can reveal traces of species that researchers may not observe directly.

Question 5

Difficult

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • A museum opened an exhibition of regional woven textiles in 1998.
  • The original labels named the collectors who had donated the textiles.
  • The original labels rarely identified the weavers.
  • In 2016, archivists linked many textiles to specific workshops and revised the labels to name those workshops.
  • For a 2024 redesign, the museum collaborated with weaving communities.
  • The 2024 labels identify individual makers when known and explain techniques in terms selected with community advisers.
Skeleton: 1998 donor focus → 2016 workshop attribution → 2024 maker/community voiceAnchor: changed over timeScope lock: show the direction across all three stagesTrick watch: the latest improvement alone does not describe an evolution

The student wants to describe how the exhibition's labeling approach changed over time. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. The museum's 1998 textile exhibition included objects donated by collectors, and archivists later connected many objects to particular workshops.
  2. B. Because the original labels rarely identified weavers, the museum redesigned the textile exhibition in 2024 with advice from weaving communities.
  3. C. The museum now identifies individual makers when known and explains textile techniques using language selected with community advisers.
  4. D. The labels shifted from centering donors, to naming workshops after archival research, to identifying makers and incorporating community perspectives.

Question 6

Moderate

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • The Harbor Reading Room opened in 1912 in a district where many dockworkers lived.
  • Its founder, Amara Bell, wanted the room to serve people whose work schedules prevented them from visiting the city's main library.
  • The room stayed open until midnight.
  • It lent books without charging membership fees.
  • Bell wrote that the room should make learning available to “those whose working hours have kept the city's books beyond their reach.”
Skeleton: founder goal → workers face schedule barrier → quotation states access aimAnchor: explain the goal + use Bell's quotationScope lock: connect the quotation to what the institution was meant to accomplishTrick watch: including quotation marks is not enough if the sentence leaves the goal implicit

The student wants to explain Bell's goal for the Harbor Reading Room using a quotation from Bell. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. Bell intended the Harbor Reading Room to extend access to workers excluded by ordinary library hours, making learning available to “those whose working hours have kept the city's books beyond their reach.”
  2. B. Opened in 1912, the Harbor Reading Room stayed open until midnight and charged no membership fees.
  3. C. Many dockworkers lived in the district where Amara Bell established the Harbor Reading Room.
  4. D. Bell wrote that some residents' working hours had “kept the city's books beyond their reach.”

Question 7

Difficult

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Albedo is the proportion of incoming light that a surface reflects.
  • Researchers measured albedo at a desert solar facility before and after dust accumulated on its panels.
  • Dust reduced the panels' electrical output by 14 percent.
  • The dusty panels reflected more incoming light than the clean panels did.
  • Researchers had expected the darker-looking dusty panels to reflect less light.
  • The team proposed that the dust's mineral composition may explain the result.
Skeleton: expected lower reflection → observed higher reflection; output also fellAnchor: unexpected finding + familiar audienceScope lock: state the result and why it was unexpected; do not spend space defining albedoTrick watch: background definitions and plausible explanations are not the surprising result itself

The student wants to present the study's unexpected finding to an audience already familiar with albedo. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. Albedo, the proportion of incoming light reflected by a surface, can be measured at desert solar facilities before and after dust accumulates.
  2. B. Although dust reduced electrical output, the dusty panels had higher albedo than clean panels—opposite the researchers' expectation for the darker-looking surface.
  3. C. Researchers proposed that the mineral composition of desert dust may affect how much incoming light solar panels reflect.
  4. D. Dust accumulation reduced the solar panels' electrical output by 14 percent and made the panels appear darker.

Question 8

Difficult

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Moonvine flowers usually open at night.
  • The humidity hypothesis proposes that opening at night protects the flowers' pollen from drying.
  • The pollinator-timing hypothesis proposes that the flowers open when their primary moth pollinators are active.
  • In a humidity-controlled greenhouse, moonvine flowers still opened after sunset.
  • When researchers shifted the moths' active period earlier by changing the light cycle, newly produced flowers began opening earlier as well.
  • Neither experiment measured pollen loss in natural conditions.
Skeleton: hypothesis 1: humidity; hypothesis 2: moth timing → two experiments differ in fitAnchor: distinguish + which one more directly supportedScope lock: define both accounts, then use cautious evidential strengthTrick watch: more direct support is not the same as proving one cause or disproving every alternative

The student wants to distinguish the two explanations and indicate which one the experiments more directly support. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. Moonvine flowers usually open at night, when humidity is higher and moth pollinators are active.
  2. B. Because the experiments did not measure pollen loss in nature, they provide no evidence relevant to either explanation.
  3. C. The humidity hypothesis links nighttime opening to reduced pollen drying, whereas the pollinator-timing hypothesis links it to moth activity; unchanged opening under controlled humidity and shifted opening with moth activity more directly support the latter.
  4. D. The greenhouse experiment disproved the humidity hypothesis, and the light-cycle experiment proved that moth activity is the only cause of flower opening.

Question 9

Difficult

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Perovskite solar cells can be manufactured at lower temperatures than conventional silicon cells.
  • Many perovskite cells lose efficiency when exposed to moisture.
  • Engineer Nia Okafor's team developed a transparent protective coating.
  • Coated cells retained 92 percent of their initial efficiency after 1,000 hours in humid air.
  • Uncoated cells retained 61 percent.
  • The coating can be applied below 100°C, a temperature compatible with flexible plastic substrates.
Skeleton: coating → better humidity retention + low-temperature application → flexible-device relevanceAnchor: practical significance for manufacturing flexible devicesScope lock: connect performance and production compatibility to the stated applicationTrick watch: a strong numerical result may still omit why the result matters for the target use

The student wants to emphasize the coating's practical significance for manufacturing flexible solar devices. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. Perovskite solar cells can be manufactured at lower temperatures than conventional silicon cells but often lose efficiency in moisture.
  2. B. Okafor's team compared coated and uncoated perovskite cells after exposing both to humid air for 1,000 hours.
  3. C. The coated cells retained 92 percent of their initial efficiency, while the uncoated cells retained 61 percent.
  4. D. The coating both improves moisture resistance and can be applied at a temperature suitable for plastic substrates, addressing two requirements for durable flexible solar devices.

Question 10

Difficult

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • A city museum surveyed 640 residents between ages 18 and 30.
  • Respondents reported whether they followed the museum's social-media account.
  • They also reported how many times they had visited the museum during the previous year.
  • Respondents who followed the account reported an average of 2.8 visits.
  • Respondents who did not follow it reported an average of 1.6 visits.
  • The survey did not determine whether following the account caused additional visits.
  • The survey did not include residents outside the 18-to-30 age range.
Skeleton: followers report 2.8 vs nonfollowers 1.6 → observational survey → age limitAnchor: qualified conclusion about relationshipScope lock: association, surveyed age group, no causal upgradeTrick watch: a directionally correct sentence can still overclaim cause or population scope

The student wants to make a qualified conclusion about the relationship between following the museum online and visiting it in person. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  1. A. Following the museum's social-media account causes residents to visit the museum 1.2 more times per year.
  2. B. Among the surveyed 18- to 30-year-olds, following the museum's account was associated with more reported visits, though the survey cannot establish that following caused the difference.
  3. C. Residents who visited the museum frequently were more likely to follow its social-media account because the account encouraged additional visits.
  4. D. The survey shows that social-media use increases museum attendance among residents of every age.
