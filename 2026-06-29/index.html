Class review

The English review preserved from the class record.

Class Content Summary

  • The original plan for this class changed because Joy found the previous grammar handout homework difficult. Instead of moving forward into advanced punctuation and the next grammar topic, we used the class to repair the tense problems that appeared in her homework.
  • The class focused on a basic tense grammar session. We reviewed how to decide between simple past, present perfect, past perfect, past continuous, and future forms by looking for time clues and sentence logic.
  • The most important focus was perfect tense, especially the difference between present perfect and past perfect. We practiced recognizing clues such as for, since, by the time, before, and earlier.
  • Advanced punctuation was postponed because Joy needs a stronger foundation in tense before adding another abstract grammar layer.

Student Performance

  • Joy admitted that this grammar material is difficult. This is understandable because tense theory, especially perfect tense, is abstract and depends on time relationships rather than simple memorization.
  • Even though the content was challenging, Joy was trying her best to digest it. She was willing to slow down, look back at the sentence, and think through why a form such as had collected or has helped was needed.
  • Her present tense and future tense foundation is relatively solid. She is more comfortable with straightforward present and future forms than with perfect tense or past-time sequence.
  • The main current difficulty is not effort; it is processing speed and comfort with abstract tense logic. Joy needs a little more time and repeated examples before this grammar becomes stable.

Improvement Plan

  • We will slow down slightly and give Joy more time to build the tense foundation before returning to advanced punctuation.
  • Future tense review should be light because Joy is already doing well there. The main extra training should focus on present perfect, past perfect, and how to read time relationships in long SAT-style sentences.
  • For each tense question, Joy should first identify the time clue, then place the actions on a timeline, and only then choose the verb form.
  • When Joy misses a tense question, the correction should include a short rule sentence such as: "This action happened before another past action, so use past perfect."

Homework

  • Review the 0629 grammar mistake explanations, especially the examples involving present perfect and past perfect.
  • Practice identifying time clues before choosing tense: for, since, by the time, before, earlier, and specific past dates.
  • Advanced punctuation practice can wait until the tense foundation is more comfortable.
