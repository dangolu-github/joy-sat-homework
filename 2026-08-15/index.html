Class review

A concise recap of the completed mistake explanations and the science-reading routine.

August 15
Class contentWe reviewed the two submitted Rhetorical Synthesis Intensive Practice sets and completed the explanation of all nine missed or unanswered questions. For each item, we used Goal-Verb Lock → Note Filter → Sentence Fit Test to identify why a true or relevant-looking choice could still miss the requested goal. We then worked through Natural Science: From Study Design to SAT Decisions, focusing on the wildlife passage. We mapped the usual science-passage flow—Question → Design → Result → Limit → Claim—and compared the two main methodological routes: a controlled experiment and an observational study.
Student performanceThe Rhetorical Synthesis sets were 20/22 and 27/34, and every identified mistake was explained. The recurring trap was choosing a sentence that contained a true note but did not preserve the exact goal, relationship, emphasis, or scope. In the wildlife passage, the key reading move was to identify the method before judging the claim: experiments can support a causal conclusion within tested conditions, while observational studies usually support an association and must keep alternative explanations visible.
Improvement planBefore checking Rhetorical Synthesis options, write: “The answer must [goal verb] [exact target] [required relationship or scope].” In a science passage, label the question, design, result, limit, and claim, then ask whether the researchers assigned a treatment or observed naturally occurring conditions. Reject the usual traps: turning association into causation, treating statistical checks as equal to random assignment, or extending an experimental result beyond the tested population or conditions.
