Class review

What we completed, the reading habits Joy used well, and the method to make more automatic.

August 3
Class contentWe completed Cross-Text Connections Part 2 with the Response + Reason Predictor. For each text, we separated the claim, reason, and limitation; identified the exact target claim; predicted whether the responder would agree, disagree, or qualify; anchored the response in the responder’s own reasoning; and preserved the scope or strength boundary. We also used “yes, and” and “yes, but” to distinguish support or complement from qualification or challenge.
What went wellJoy remained engaged and willing to try even though she was not feeling well. She already uses a helpful question-first reading habit before moving through Text 1 and Text 2, and she could identify surface-level disagreement and obvious subject-matter mismatches.
Main challengeThe finer relationship was not yet consistent. Joy found it difficult to summarize each text’s claim, reason, and limitation concisely; distinguish a challenge to the claim from a challenge to the reasoning or a qualification of scope; and reject choices that reused the right keywords while reversing the relationship or pairing the right polarity with the wrong reason.
Improvement planFor every pair, write three short lines—Claim / Reason / Limit—before comparing the texts. Then name the relationship with “yes, and” or “yes, but,” state whether it affects the claim, reasoning, or scope, and test each option against target / polarity / reason / boundary. We will also build quicker recognition of experiments, observations, theories, and statistics so that evidence strength can be judged more efficiently.
HomeworkComplete HM1: 30 independent Response + Reason Predictor questions. Keep the Claim / Reason / Limit notes short, record the target, polarity, reason, and boundary before choosing, and mark any two close options for review. Corrections and explanations will be released after the independent attempt.
