Class review

The English review preserved from the class record.

Class Content Summary

  • Class 4 began with a review of Joy's punctuation handout mistakes. We used the missed questions to reinforce a more reliable method: first identify the sentence structure, then decide whether punctuation is needed.
  • We also finished discussing the grammar handout, especially perfect tenses and parallel verb structure. These points connect directly to SAT Form, Structure, and Sense questions because Joy needs to separate real main verbs from verb forms inside modifiers or extra information.
  • The class then moved into a more detailed punctuation lesson. We started with comma use and focused on when commas mark removable information, when they separate sentence parts, and when no comma should be added inside one grammar unit.

Student Performance

  • Tense and punctuation questions are theory-heavy, so this part of SAT grammar was naturally less engaging. Joy also said that she can get lost during this kind of material because the knowledge itself is not very interesting.
  • Even with that, Joy managed to stay with the class flow most of the time. She responded well when the tutor redirected her from choosing by “flow” or “pause” to choosing by sentence structure.
  • A clear strength is that Joy can self-correct when given a cleaner framework. For example, after reviewing a punctuation question again through structure, she was able to move away from an answer that only sounded smoother.
  • Joy also showed useful intuition about verb forms. She could notice that a word that looks like a verb may actually be part of a modifier or passive structure rather than the main verb.
  • The main weakness from this class is sentence-structure identification. Joy was often distracted by modifiers, appositives, parenthetical information, and prepositional phrases, which made it harder to find the true subject, main verb, and object.
  • Dash use for interruption, appositive punctuation, and the difference between semicolon/conjunction logic still need more review. Small careless punctuation mistakes also suggest that one quick reread before answering would help.

Improvement Plan

  • For complex sentences, Joy should first mark the subject, main verb, object if there is one, modifier phrases, and any parenthetical or appositive structure.
  • After marking the parts, she should shorten the sentence to its skeleton. The goal is to keep only the core sentence and remove extra information before choosing punctuation.
  • When choosing punctuation, the decision should come from structure rather than how the sentence sounds when read aloud.
  • The new sentence-structure handout should be used as targeted practice. It directly trains the weakness seen in class: finding the real subject and verb inside a long SAT-style sentence.
  • For punctuation questions, Joy should reread the sentence once before finalizing the answer to catch small comma, dash, or no-punctuation mistakes.

Homework

  • Finish the grammar handout.
  • Finish the sentence-structure / sentence-skeleton handout.
  • Vocabulary Group 1 is still optional. The vocabulary practice questions are not required unless Joy wants extra vocabulary work.
