Sentence Structure Skeleton Practice

After-Class Supplement: subject, verb, object, modifiers, parenthesis/appositive, and shortest sentence core

Sentence Shortening Method

Subject
Verb
Object
Modifier
Parenthesis
  1. Find the finite verb first. Look for the verb that changes tense: reopened, has improved, was moved.
  2. Ask who or what does that verb. That is the main subject.
  3. Ask what receives the action. That is the object if the verb has one.
  4. Put time, place, reason, manner, and prepositional phrases into the modifier box.
  5. Put appositives, nonessential clauses, and removable middle phrases into the parenthesis box.
  6. Write the shortest sentence that keeps the main idea.

Worked Example

After several failed trials, the small battery prototype, a model built from recycled parts, powered the emergency radio for six hours.

Subjectthe small battery prototype
Verbpowered
Objectthe emergency radio
ModifiersAfter several failed trials; for six hours
Parenthesis / Appositivea model built from recycled parts
SkeletonThe prototype powered the radio.

Student Directions

  1. Do not translate the whole sentence first.
  2. Find the finite verb, then find the subject that controls it.
  3. Separate removable information from the sentence core.
  4. Write a short skeleton sentence. It should still be grammatical.
