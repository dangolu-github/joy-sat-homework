Class review

A concise recap of the grammar repair, science-text work, and transition review.

August 11
Class contentWe reviewed several errors from the earlier grammar practice, including conjunctive adverbs such as even so, time adverbs such as later and prior to, semicolon use, paired commas, and modifier targets. In Research and Science Texts, we covered only Part 1, “See the study before judging the claim.” This section separates the five study fields, distinguishes hypothesis, prediction, and result, identifies explanatory, response, controlled, and confounding variables, separates comparison groups from controlled variables, and contrasts experiments with observational studies. The class also used the relationship-first method from the Transitions section of the combined review.
Student performanceJoy could explain that a semicolon can join two independent clauses and could identify the groups being compared in a science example. With prompting, she also recognized that even so works like however. She still needed support to explain the exact rule instead of relying on what looked right, and the differences among research questions, hypotheses, variables, and research stages were not yet automatic.
Improvement planFor grammar, classify the sentence parts and the connector before choosing punctuation, and place an opening modifier directly beside its logical actor. For Part 1 science texts, keep the study roles separate: question or hypothesis → design → result → interpretation → limit; then label the explanatory and response variables, the baseline comparison group, and the conditions held constant. For transitions, state the relationship between the ideas before evaluating the connector.
