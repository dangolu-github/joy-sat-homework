Class review

What the grammar review covered, what Joy could already apply, and what should become more automatic.

August 7
Class contentWe reviewed how sentence structure controls grammar choices. The main focus was finding the head subject and finite verb in long sentences, separating finite verbs from infinitives, gerunds, and participles, and deciding when a sentence needs active or passive voice. We also reviewed subject–verb agreement in “A of B” structures, subject and object pronouns, clear antecedents, singular and plural possession, subordinating conjunctions, and nonessential which clauses.
Student performanceJoy correctly formed the plural possessive scientists', selected their once the antecedent rule was explicit, and recognised that fragments or a collection receive the action rather than performing it. She still needed prompting to choose object pronouns, explain what subordinating conjunctions connect, distinguish a participle from a finite verb, build the correct passive tense, identify the head noun in a modified subject, and match a singular subject with stands or provides.
Improvement planFor every long sentence, Joy should circle the verb-like forms, bracket modifiers, and reduce the clause to its shortest core. She should then underline the head subject, apply the A of B rule, decide whether the subject performs or receives the action, and check agreement, tense, pronoun case, antecedent, and clause status in that order. The goal is faster independent recall, not another broad grammar lecture.
