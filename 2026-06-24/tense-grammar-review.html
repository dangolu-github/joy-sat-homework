1. Present Simple

I / you / we / they + base verb

he / she / it / singular noun + -s verb

Use present simple for general facts, repeated actions, definitions, habits, and the normal function of a thing.

The earth goes around the sun.

This tool measures distance.

Museums often display objects from different periods.

Practice 1A

Complete the sentences with the present simple form.

  1. The river into the sea. (flow)
  2. This machine small changes in pressure. (detect)
  3. Many students notes during class. (take)
  4. The museum at 5 p.m. on Fridays. (close)
  5. Plants light to make energy. (use)

2. Present Continuous

am / is / are + verb-ing

Use present continuous for an action happening now, around now, or during a temporary period. It can also show a change that is happening.

The students are writing a lab report right now.

The city is building a new bridge this year.

The weather is getting colder.

Practice 2A

Complete the sentences with the present continuous form.

  1. Please be quiet. I to concentrate. (try)
  2. The team water samples this week. (test)
  3. Look at the sky. It darker. (get)
  4. The students a short article now. (read)
  5. The company a new website this month. (design)

4. Past Simple

regular: verb-ed irregular: wrote, made, went, saw

Use past simple for a finished action in the past, especially with a finished time expression.

The museum opened in 1931.

The scientist wrote the report last year.

They visited the archive yesterday.

5. Past Continuous

was / were + verb-ing

Use past continuous for an action that was in progress at a time in the past. It often gives background for a shorter past action.

She was reading when the phone rang.

The researchers were recording data when the lights went out.

6. Present Perfect

has / have + past participle

Use present perfect when a past action connects to the present. The exact finished past time is not the focus.

Scientists have studied the site for decades.

The archive has helped many researchers since it opened.

The city has added several bike lanes over the past decade.

  5. The weather a lot recently. (change)

8. Past Perfect

had + past participle

Use past perfect for the earlier of two past actions.

By the time the team published the report, it had collected three years of data.

The students understood the article because they had reviewed the vocabulary earlier.

9. Passive Tense Forms

be + past participle

Use passive voice when the subject receives the action. The be verb shows tense and agreement.

TenseActivePassive
Present simpleResearchers use the method.The method is used.
Past simpleArtists painted the murals.The murals were painted.
Present perfectScholars have copied the manuscript.The manuscript has been copied.
Past perfectThe team had recorded the interviews.The interviews had been recorded.

Practice 9A

Complete the sentences with the passive form.

  1. The method by many laboratories today. (use)
  2. The murals by local artists in the 1970s. (paint)
  3. The manuscript several times. (copy, present perfect passive)
  4. The interviews before the article was written. (record, past perfect passive)
  5. The instruments before every experiment. (calibrate)

10. Future Time Clauses

After when, if, before, after, and until, use present tense for future meaning.

The software will save the file when the upload finishes.

If the weather is clear tomorrow, we will collect samples.
