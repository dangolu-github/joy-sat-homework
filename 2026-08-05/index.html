Class review

What we covered, what Joy could already do, and what she should practise next.

August 5
Class contentWe reviewed the grammar behind Sentence Boundaries. Joy practised finding the subject and finite verb, separating phrases from clauses, and deciding whether a clause was independent or dependent. We then applied those labels to legal joins between independent clauses, comma plus FANBOYS, conjunctive-adverb punctuation, and dependent-clause attachment.
Student performanceJoy took part throughout the review and asked to revisit punctuation patterns that still felt uncertain. In a simple sentence, she could identify the subject, finite verb, and object, and she corrected a misunderstanding when it arose. Longer sentences were less secure. Modifying phrases sometimes hid the main clause, and her choice between a comma, semicolon, or no mark was inconsistent.
Improvement planBefore choosing punctuation, Joy should mark the subject and finite verb on both sides, label each side IC, DC, or phrase, and name the job of the boundary. The next practice will focus on legal joins between independent clauses and the semicolon plus conjunctive adverb plus comma pattern. Short recall checks will show whether she can use each rule without prompting.
HomeworkComplete Intensive Practice > Standard English Conventions > Boundaries. Start with Practice 1 and continue through the sets in order. Use the Class 20 handout while working, and write the clause labels before choosing a punctuation mark.
