Class review

A concise recap of the checked work and the next repair routine.

August 20
Class contentWe reviewed Joy’s submitted mistakes in Boundaries Practice 3 and Form, Structure, and Sense Practices 1 and 3. We then worked through modifier structure, including the target immediately after an opening modifier, dummy there, and the difference between a nearby noun and the true head subject. In Natural Science: What Can a Measurement Show?, we covered the eight Vocabulary Bridge terms: empirical, variable, control, confound, replicate, calibrate, precision, and uncertainty.
Student performanceThe submitted results were 13/24 in Boundaries Practice 3, 21/26 in Form, Structure, and Sense Practice 1, and 13/21 in Practice 3. The pattern shows that punctuation was sometimes chosen before the sentence units were classified. In Form, Structure, and Sense, the strongest repeated issue was locking an opening modifier to its true target; identifying the head subject was a secondary issue.
Improvement planFor Boundaries, mark the subjects and finite verbs, label each unit IC, DC, or phrase, and only then choose punctuation. For an opening modifier, ask who performs the action or has the quality, and place that target immediately after the modifier. Review each science term through its boundary: for example, precision is not the same as accuracy, and a confound supplies another possible explanation rather than proving one cause.
