1. Precise Transition Function

The Green Line theater was built to make experimental dance visible outside major arts districts; fittingly, its lobby doubles as a rehearsal space whose glass walls allow pedestrians to watch performers prepare before each show.

  • Idea before transition: the theater's purpose is public visibility for dance.
  • Idea after transition: the building design makes rehearsal visible to pedestrians.
  • Exact move: the second fact suits the stated purpose.
  • Why this is Class 9: for example would be too flat; fittingly explains the rhetorical match.

2. Concession Versus Replacement

The first version of the map included only entrances on the north side of campus; still, because it marked every accessible ramp on that side, it helped planners identify where temporary signs would be most useful during orientation week.

  • Idea before transition: the map has limited coverage.
  • Idea after transition: the limited map remains useful for a specific planning job.
  • Exact move: concession, not replacement.
  • Control check: instead would require a "not this, but that" structure, which the sentence does not have.

3. Evidence To Inference

During the restoration, technicians found the same blue pigment beneath three layers of later paint and on two panels that had been stored separately for decades; therefore, they concluded that the panels had originally belonged to the same room.

  • Evidence: same pigment appears in multiple independent locations.
  • Conclusion: the panels likely came from the same room.
  • Exact move: the transition connects a body of evidence to a justified inference.
  • Control check: the second clause is not an example; it is the conclusion drawn from the evidence.

4. Rhetorical Synthesis From Notes

Notes: The Harbor Lab project collects water samples from six docks. It publishes results in a free online dashboard. Local high school students help gather data every Saturday. The dashboard shows changes in salinity and temperature.

Goal: Emphasize that the project connects public science with student participation.

Strong synthesis: The Harbor Lab project combines public science with student participation by publishing free water-quality data online and having local high school students help gather samples each Saturday.

  • Selected notes: free online data + student sampling.
  • Omitted note: six docks is true but not necessary for the stated goal.
  • Exact move: choose relevant notes, then combine them around the goal.

5. Sentence Combining For Purpose

Original ideas: Engineer Priya Nair designed a low-cost air monitor. The monitor can be assembled from parts found in most school labs. The goal is to emphasize why the invention is practical for classrooms.

Combined sentence: Engineer Priya Nair designed a low-cost air monitor, a practical classroom tool that can be assembled from parts found in most school labs.

  • Main clause: Nair designed the monitor.
  • Appositive: a practical classroom tool.
  • Relative clause: that can be assembled from common school-lab parts.
  • Exact move: the main idea stays clear while the extra detail explains practical value.
