Joy SAT Lesson 09: Transitions II and Rhetorical Synthesis

0708 Class 9

Today's Goal

  • Use precise transition words when two answer choices share the same broad relationship.
  • Separate contrast, concession, replacement, comparison, and final inference.
  • Turn notes into one sentence that matches a stated purpose.
  • Combine ideas without adding irrelevant details or changing the intended emphasis.
Class 9 focus: Do not stop at "same direction" or "opposite direction." Ask what the second idea is doing: proving, limiting, replacing, clarifying, comparing, or concluding.

1. Precision Inside One Transition Family

Some transitions belong to the same broad direction but do different jobs. The SAT often hides the answer by giving two choices that both sound logical.

TransitionPrecise jobBest cue
in fact / indeedstrengthens or makes the previous claim more exactnumbers, sharper evidence, stronger wording
specificallynarrows a broad category to one detailgeneral word followed by a named detail
fittinglyshows that the second fact suits the first facta result that feels appropriate or matched
ultimatelymoves to final purpose, final result, or deeper meaningsurface detail followed by real goal or final outcome
Recognition cue: If two choices both continue the idea, ask which one describes the writer's exact move.
Example
  • Idea 1: The festival was designed to bring residents into public courtyards.
  • Idea 2: The opening event took place in the oldest courtyard in the neighborhood.
  • Relation between ideas: The event matches the purpose of the festival.
  • Transition: Fittingly
  • Final sentence: The festival was designed to bring residents into public courtyards. Fittingly, the opening event took place in the oldest courtyard in the neighborhood.
Weak choice: For example only says the second idea is one case.
Stronger choice: Fittingly explains why that case is especially appropriate.
Takeaway: In Class 9, correct answers often depend on the exact rhetorical job, not only the broad relationship.
Practice 1.1 (Easy)
Precision

The memorial garden was created to honor local musicians. ______ its central path is lined with stones engraved with song titles from the town's oldest choir.

A. Fittingly,
B. However,
C. As a result,
D. Meanwhile,
Practice 1.2 (Difficult)
Precision

The museum's label identifies the clay figure as a household object; ______ chemical testing suggests that it may have been used in ritual offerings rather than ordinary domestic work.

A. similarly,
B. ultimately,
C. nevertheless,
D. specifically,

2. Contrast, Concession, Replacement, And Comparison

These choices can feel similar because they all point away from a simple continuation. The exact movement is different.

MoveMeaningCommon transitions
ContrastA and B point in different directionshowever, by contrast, conversely
ConcessionA is true, but B limits itstill, nevertheless, granted, that said
Replacementnot A; B insteadinstead, rather
ComparisonB is measured beside Aby comparison, similarly, likewise
Recognition cue: Look for the grammar of the idea: "not A but B" usually wants replacement; "A is true but limited" usually wants concession.
Example
  • Idea 1: The trial included only two clinics.
  • Idea 2: The improvement appeared in both clinics and across several age groups.
  • Relation between ideas: The evidence has a limitation, but it still has value.
  • Transition: Still
  • Final sentence: The trial included only two clinics. Still, the improvement appeared in both clinics and across several age groups.
Weak choice: Instead would suggest that the second idea replaces the first idea.
Stronger choice: Still shows concession: the limitation remains, but the result matters.
Takeaway: Contrast is difference; concession is difference plus "even so"; replacement is "not this, but that."
Practice 2.1 (Easy)
Concession

The first survey included only weekend visitors. ______ its findings helped planners identify the park entrances that needed clearer signs.

A. Still,
B. Instead,
C. Similarly,
D. Therefore,
Practice 2.2 (Difficult)
Replacement

The committee did not base the award on the number of published articles alone. ______ it considered whether the research had changed how other scholars interpreted the archive.

A. By comparison,
B. Instead,
C. Granted,

3. Inference Transitions Across A Whole Sentence

Inference transitions do not just connect two nearby nouns. They connect evidence to a conclusion. Before choosing therefore, accordingly, or consequently, identify what conclusion the writer is allowed to draw.

Recognition cue: Ask: "What does the evidence prove?" The answer after the blank should be a conclusion, decision, or expected result.
Example
  • Idea 1: Three independent sensors recorded the same pressure drop.
  • Idea 2: The team ruled out instrument error.
  • Relation between ideas: Repeated evidence supports a conclusion.
  • Transition: Therefore
  • Final sentence: Three independent sensors recorded the same pressure drop. Therefore, the team ruled out instrument error.
Weak choice: For example would treat the conclusion as one example of sensor data.
Stronger choice: Therefore marks the conclusion drawn from the evidence.
Takeaway: Inference transitions answer "so what follows from the evidence?"
Practice 3.1 (Easy)
Inference

Every soil sample from the lower field contained the same trace mineral. ______ the research team concluded that the field had once been covered by the same streambed.

A. Therefore,
B. For instance,
C. Nevertheless,
D. Similarly,
Practice 3.2 (Difficult)
Inference

The revised evacuation map shortened the route from the west dormitory by 400 meters and avoided the narrow bridge that had delayed drills in the past. The new map, ______ should reduce crowding during future practice drills.

A. for example,
B. nevertheless,
C. in other words,
D. accordingly,

4. Rhetorical Synthesis From Notes

Rhetorical synthesis questions give notes and a goal. Do not try to include every note. Choose the note or notes that directly satisfy the goal.

  1. Read the goal first.
  2. Choose the useful note group.
  3. Remove facts that do not help the goal.
  4. Combine the selected notes into one precise sentence.
Recognition cue: The best answer may leave out true information if that information does not match the task.
Example

Notes: The Juniper Street mural was painted in 1988. It shows workers from three nearby factories. It was restored by volunteers in 2024.

Goal: Emphasize the mural's community connection.

  • Idea 1: The mural shows workers from local factories.
  • Idea 2: Volunteers restored it.
  • Relation between ideas: Both details connect the mural to local people.
  • Transition: and
  • Final sentence: The Juniper Street mural reflects the neighborhood's community history, showing workers from three nearby factories and having been restored by local volunteers in 2024.
Weak answer: A sentence that only gives the 1988 date may be true but misses the community goal.
Stronger answer: A sentence selects the notes that prove community connection.
Takeaway: In synthesis, relevance to the goal matters more than using every note.
Practice 4.1 (Easy)
Synthesis

Notes: The Aster app identifies native plants. It was designed by college students. It includes audio descriptions for users with low vision. Goal: Highlight the app's accessibility feature.

A. The Aster app, designed by college students, identifies native plants.
B. The Aster app identifies native plants and includes audio descriptions for users with low vision.
C. College students designed the Aster app after studying native plants.
D. Native plants can be identified using the Aster app.
Practice 4.2 (Difficult)
Synthesis

Notes: Poet Ada Limon wrote In Praise of Mystery. The poem was engraved on a spacecraft launched in 2024. The spacecraft is headed toward Jupiter's moon Europa. The poem addresses the idea of wonder across distance. Goal: Introduce the poem by connecting its theme to its unusual placement.

A. Ada Limon wrote In Praise of Mystery, which was engraved on a spacecraft launched in 2024.
B. A spacecraft launched in 2024 is traveling toward Europa, one of Jupiter's moons.
C. Fittingly, Ada Limon's In Praise of Mystery, a poem about wonder across distance, was engraved on a spacecraft traveling toward Europa.
D. In Praise of Mystery is one of several works connected to space exploration.

5. Sentence Combining For Purpose And Audience

Sentence combining is not only grammar. The best combined sentence controls emphasis. Put the most important idea in the main clause, and place background information in a phrase, appositive, or relative clause.

Recognition cue: If the goal is to emphasize an action, make the action the main verb. If the goal is to identify a person or object, use an appositive or relative clause.
Example
  • Idea 1: Dr. Mira Chen developed a low-cost water sensor.
  • Idea 2: The sensor can be repaired with common parts.
  • Relation between ideas: Idea 2 explains why the invention is practical.
  • Transition/structure: appositive + relative clause
  • Final sentence: Dr. Mira Chen developed a low-cost water sensor, a practical device that can be repaired with common parts.
Weak combination: Dr. Mira Chen can be repaired with common parts.
Stronger combination: The modifier clearly describes the device, not the person.
Takeaway: Combine ideas so the main clause carries the purpose and every modifier points to the right target.
Practice 5.1 (Easy)
Combining

Which choice best combines the sentences while emphasizing the purpose of the library program? The library program lends telescopes. The program is designed to make astronomy equipment available to families who cannot buy it.

A. The library program lends telescopes, making astronomy equipment available to families who cannot buy it.
B. Families who cannot buy astronomy equipment can use a library program, and telescopes are equipment.
C. The library program, which is a program, lends telescopes to astronomy.
D. Telescopes are lent by the program, which families cannot buy.
Practice 5.2 (Difficult)
Combining

Which choice best combines the sentences while keeping the scientist's finding as the main idea? Marine ecologist Leila Grant studied kelp beds near shipping lanes. She found that noise from boat traffic changed how young fish gathered around the kelp.

A. Marine ecologist Leila Grant studied kelp beds near shipping lanes, and she was a scientist.
B. While studying kelp beds near shipping lanes, marine ecologist Leila Grant found that boat-traffic noise changed how young fish gathered around the kelp.
C. Kelp beds near shipping lanes were studied by Leila Grant because young fish gathered around the kelp.
D. Young fish gathered around kelp, and marine ecologist Leila Grant studied shipping lanes.

Class 9 Checklist

  1. Name the exact move: prove, limit, replace, compare, clarify, or conclude.
  2. Check whether a transition is too broad for the sentence's purpose.
  3. For notes questions, read the goal before the notes.
  4. Select only the notes that serve the goal.
  5. In combined sentences, place the main point in the main clause.
