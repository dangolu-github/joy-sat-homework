Core Method

Before choosing a verb, do three checks:

  1. Function: Does the sentence need a real main verb, or a nonfinite phrase?
  2. Time: What time clue controls the action?
  3. Agreement: What is the real subject?

This is the same structure-first habit from Lesson 1, but now the target is verb form instead of punctuation.

1. Finite Verbs vs Nonfinite Verb Forms

A finite verb can be the main verb of a clause. It changes for tense or subject.

A nonfinite verb cannot be the main verb by itself. It usually works as a purpose phrase, modifier, or noun-like phrase.

Type Forms To Memorize Example SAT Use
Finite measures, measured, was, had measured The tool measures quality of life. Can complete the main sentence.
Infinitive to + base verb Ochoa left NASA research to join astronaut training. Often shows purpose: left to do what?
-ing form verb + ing The singer gained fame, earning a national nickname. Often adds descriptive result or extra information.
Past participle built, written, known, used The battery invented earlier held less charge. Often describes a noun; may also form passive voice.
Demonstration: Botanists recognize many sunflower species. One species, the silverleaf sunflower, includes two ecotypes.
The subject is One species. The sentence still needs a main verb, so includes is correct, not including or to include.

2. Simple Present

Subject + base verb / -s verb

Use simple present for general facts, repeated actions, definitions, current functions, and things that remain true.

Subject Verb Form Example
I / you / we / they / plural noun measure, enable, remain Sound-based tools enable scientists to detect patterns.
he / she / it / singular noun measures, enables, remains This tool measures economic potential.
Common SAT trap: The noun closest to the verb may not be the subject.
The Arrhenius equation, named for its creator, remains important.
Subject = equation, not creator.

3. Present Progressive

am / is / are + verb-ing

Use present progressive for an action happening now or around the current moment. SAT often uses this form as a wrong answer when the sentence states a general fact.

Correct: The researcher is analyzing the samples this week.
Action is happening around now.
Usually wrong for general fact: The index is measuring quality of life.
Better: The index measures quality of life.

4. Present Perfect

has / have + past participle

Use present perfect for an action that started in the past and connects to the present, or for an action completed at an unspecified time before now.

Use Example Time Meaning
Past to present Scientists have studied the site for decades. The study began in the past and continues or matters now.
Unspecified past The theory has influenced later research. The exact date is not the focus.
SAT warning: Do not use present perfect for a finished historical action with a specific past date.
Lincoln has appointed his cabinet in 1860. Wrong.
Lincoln appointed his cabinet in 1860. Correct.

5. Simple Past

regular: verb-ed | irregular: wrote, made, was, were, became

Use simple past for a completed action in the past. SAT time clues often include a year, a historical period, or a phrase like in 1881, in the 1970s, or from 1440 to 1473.

Historical date: In 1881, Faure redesigned the battery.
Past period: The Kingdom of Benin was ruled by Oba Ewuare I from 1440 to 1473.

6. Past Progressive

was / were + verb-ing

Use past progressive for an action that was ongoing at a specific time in the past, often when another shorter action interrupted it.

Example: The researchers were testing the device when the power failed.
Ongoing background action = were testing. Shorter interrupting action = failed.
SAT warning: Do not use past progressive when the sentence needs a completed earlier action.
Misaka was already serving in World War II when he joined the Knicks. This suggests the service was still happening at that later moment.
Misaka had already served in World War II when he joined the Knicks. Correct if the service happened before he joined.

7. Past Perfect

had + past participle

Use past perfect for the earlier of two past actions. This is one of the most important SAT tense patterns.

Timeline Sentence Why
Planté invented first. Faure redesigned later. Faure redesigned the battery that Planté had invented earlier. The invention happened before the redesign.
Misaka served first. Misaka joined the Knicks later. Misaka had already served in World War II when he joined the Knicks. The service happened before he joined.
Time clues: by the time, before, earlier, already, previously, after.

8. Future And Future-In-The-Past

will + base verb

would + base verb

Use will for a future action from the present point of view. Use would when the sentence looks from a past point of view toward a later event.

Future from now: The committee will publish the report next year.
Future from a past point: In 1994, the organization would acknowledge Wang Zhenyi's contributions.

SAT rarely needs complicated future perfect forms. If a future-perfect answer appears, it is often a distractor unless the timeline clearly requires it.

9. Passive Voice

be + past participle

Use passive voice when the subject receives the action. SAT tests whether the passive verb still agrees with the subject and matches the time clue.

Tense Active Passive
Simple present Researchers use the method. The method is used by researchers.
Simple past Oba Ewuare ruled the kingdom. The kingdom was ruled by Oba Ewuare.
Present perfect Scientists have classified the animal. The animal has been classified.
Past perfect Planté had invented the battery. The battery had been invented earlier.

10. Parallel Verb Form

When two verbs share the same subject and happen in the same time frame, they usually need matching forms.

Example: Sam Gilliam applied paint to large canvases and suspended them from ceilings.
Same subject = Sam Gilliam. Same time frame = late 1960s. Matching past-tense verbs = applied and suspended.
Wrong pattern: He applied paint and to suspend the canvases.
The second verb does not match the first verb.

Comprehensive Mixed Grammar Practice

These are not SAT-style questions. They are grammar drills to build tense control before returning to SAT questions.

A. Fill In The Correct Verb Form

  1. This tool several measures of public well-being. (measure)
  2. In 1860, Lincoln several former rivals to his cabinet. (appoint)
  3. By the time the team published the report, it three years of data. (collect)
  4. The silverleaf sunflower two major ecotypes. (include)
  5. The researchers the samples when the equipment stopped working. (analyze)
  6. The method by many laboratories today. (use)
  7. The organization the astronomer's work almost two centuries after her death. (acknowledge, future-in-the-past)
  8. The artist applied paint to large canvases and them from the ceiling. (suspend)

B. Choose Simple Past, Present Perfect, Or Past Perfect

  1. The museum in 1931. (open)
  2. Scientists the island's birds for more than fifty years. (study)
  3. The original theory before the new evidence appeared. (change)
  4. In 1990, Ochoa her position at the research center. (leave)
  5. The archive many scholars since it became public. (help)
  6. By the time the historian wrote the article, she hundreds of letters. (read)

C. Correct The Verb Error

  1. The African Games Co-production Market promote the growth of the gaming industry.
  2. The equation, named for its creator, remain important in modern chemistry.
  3. One species of sunflower including two ecotypes.
  4. The artist applied bold colors and to suspend the canvases from the wall.
  5. Planté invents the battery fifteen years before Faure redesigned it.
  6. Lincoln has appointed his cabinet in 1860.

D. Mixed Paragraph Practice

Fill each blank with the correct form of the verb in parentheses.

In 1881, French chemist Camille Faure (redesign) the rechargeable lead-acid battery. The original battery, which Gaston Planté (invent) fifteen years earlier, (hold) less electricity than Faure's improved version. Today, rechargeable batteries (power) many everyday devices. Engineers (continue) to improve battery storage, and future designs (support) even more efficient technology.
