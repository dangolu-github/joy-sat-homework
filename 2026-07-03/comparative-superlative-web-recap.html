Comparative and Superlative · Web Recap

An original recap of the selected comparison patterns. The source textbook pages are not republished.

Comparatives

  • Use -er for many short adjectives and more + adjective for many longer adjectives.
  • Use than to name the second side of a comparison: faster than, more reliable than.
  • Keep the compared targets parallel: compare a lifespan with a lifespan, not a lifespan with an animal.

Superlatives and irregular forms

  • Use -est or most when one item is compared with a whole group.
  • Memorize irregular forms: good or well → better → best; bad or badly → worse → worst.
  • Check fixed frames such as as … as, more … than, and one of the most …

SAT check

  • Underline both comparison targets and rewrite them in the same grammatical form.
  • Reject an answer that creates a logical mismatch even if its adjective form is correct.
